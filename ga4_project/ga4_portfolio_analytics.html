GA4 Portfolio Analytics

How CV traffic translated into portfolio engagement

Project Overview

This case study analyses anonymised, aggregated GA4 data from my personal portfolio. The goal was to understand whether links shared in CVs and job applications created meaningful engagement, and where measurement could be improved.

Data is aggregated and covers December 2024 to September 2026. No personal identifiers, IP addresses or user-level journeys are shown.

What the Data Showed

CV links created a real acquisition channel. At least 72 first-time users arrived from CV and job-application sources, showing that the portfolio was opened beyond the CV itself.
Visitors explored proof of work. Case studies received 53 tracked clicks, while certificate links received 60. The next iteration should make case studies more prominent and give them clearer business-focused summaries.
Traffic quality matters more than headline volume. A meaningful share of visits came from application links, while debugging traffic and internal visits can distort results if they are not excluded.

Measurement Improvements

  • Standardise UTM parameters for every CV, application and LinkedIn link.
  • Exclude Tag Assistant and internal traffic from performance reporting.
  • Track key actions consistently: case-study click, LinkedIn click, CV download and contact-form submission.
  • Combine GA4 engagement data with Google Search Console to distinguish discovery from on-site behaviour.
Next step: build a second acquisition channel through SEO-focused insight pages and LinkedIn distribution, so portfolio visibility does not depend only on job applications.

Tools Used

Google Analytics 4, UTM parameters, event tracking, Google Search Console, HTML and CSS.
